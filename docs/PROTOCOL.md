# Desk Health Protocol

A practical, physio-informed movement protocol for all-day computer work. Assumes your desk setup is already ergonomic. This document is the **authoritative schedule** for implementers and the day-to-day guide for users.

---

## Cheat sheet

| Layer | Cadence | Duration | What to do |
|-------|---------|----------|------------|
| **A** | Every **20 min** | 30–45 s | 20-20-20 eyes + posture reset (chin tuck, shoulders drop, 5 scap squeezes) |
| **B** | Every **40 min** | 90–120 s | Stand if possible: neck → chest open → thoracic ext → hip flexors → wrists |
| **C** | Every **90 min** | 3–5 min | **Band mini-circuit** *or* **walk** (prefer outdoors / other room) + hydrate |
| **D** | Daily anchors | 2–10+ min | Start: posture + pull-aparts · Lunch: real break · End: bands + openers |

**Rules of thumb:** move often; mild tension only; skip anything sharp, dizzy, or radiating; no all-day “perfect posture” bracing.

---

## Core principles

### 1. Motion over “perfect posture”

Even a good posture becomes harmful if you hold it statically for hours. Tissues need change of load: sit, stand, lean, walk, reset. The goal is **frequent position change**, not a single ideal angle locked in all day.

### 2. Tissue tolerance

Short, frequent breaks outperform rare long ones for comfort and load management. A 40-second reset every 20 minutes is usually more protective than a single long stretch at the end of the day.

### 3. Eyes need distance

Near focus all day loads the focusing system (accommodation) and reduces blink rate. Looking far away and blinking fully is not optional fluff—it is the visual equivalent of standing up for your neck and shoulders.

### 4. Undoing the desk pattern

Desk work tends to close the front of the body and quiet the back of the body. The protocol deliberately:

- Opens the **chest**
- Retracts the **scapulae** (shoulder blades)
- Extends the **thoracic spine** (mid-back)
- Opens the **hip flexors**
- Lightly activates the **glutes / posterior chain**

### 5. Simplicity wins

Only activities that fit at or near a desk will actually happen. If you skip something, do the next layer rather than abandoning the day.

## The schedule

Implementers should treat Layers A–C as the timed core. Layer D is optional structure around the workday.

### Layer A — every 20 minutes (30–45 seconds)

Stay at the desk if needed. Do both:

1. **20-20-20 eyes**  
   Look ~**20 feet / 6 m** away for **20 seconds**. Blink fully **10 times** (soft full blinks, not hard squints).

2. **Posture reset**  
   - Sit tall (length through the spine, not rigid).  
   - Chin gently tuck: make a soft double chin **without** tilting the head down.  
   - Let shoulders drop away from the ears.  
   - **5 slow shoulder-blade squeezes** (imagine pinching a pencil between the blades; release fully between reps).

### Layer B — every 40 minutes (90–120 seconds)

**Stand preferred.** Run this short circuit **once**:

| # | Movement | Time / notes |
|---|----------|--------------|
| 1 | **Neck side bend + gentle rotation** (both sides) | ~15 s each direction; total ~45 s. Move slowly; no forcing end range. |
| 2 | **Chest opener** | Doorway stretch **or** clasp hands behind the back; **20 s**. Soften the ribs; don’t crank the low back. |
| 3 | **Thoracic extension** | Over the chair back **or** reach arms up + slight lean back; **15 s**. Mid-back focus, not neck jamming. |
| 4 | **Standing hip flexor stretch** | Both sides, **15 s each**. Tall torso; gentle tuck of pelvis if that helps you feel the front of the hip. |
| 5 | **Wrist / forearm** | Prayer stretch + reverse prayer **or** finger fans; **15 s** total. |

### Layer C — every 90 minutes (3–5 minutes)

Choose **one**:

#### Option 1 — Resistance band mini-circuit

Use a **light–moderate** band. Clean form over load.

1. Band pull-aparts × **12**  
2. Band face pulls / high pulls × **10**  
3. Seated or standing band rows × **10**  
4. Band good-mornings **or** glute bridges (if floor is OK) × **10**  
5. Optional: standing band hip abductions × **8** / side  

#### Option 2 — Walk

Leave the desk. Walk **3–5 minutes**, preferably outdoors or at least to another room. Hydrate.

### Layer D — optional daily anchors

| When | What |
|------|------|
| **Start of day** | ~2 min: posture reset + band pull-aparts |
| **Lunch** | **10+ min** real break away from the screen |
| **End of day** | ~3 min: band pull-aparts + chest openers + hip flexors |

## What **not** to do

- **No aggressive neck cracking** or forced joint “pops.”
- **No holding painful stretches.** Mild stretch sensation is fine; sharp pain is a stop signal.
- **No “max posture” bracing all day.** Tall and soft beats rigid military posture for hours.
- **Skip or modify** anything that causes **sharp pain, dizziness, or numbness/tingling radiating** down the arms or legs—and see a clinician if those show up.

---

## Intensity guide

| Domain | Target | Stop if |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Stretches** | Mild, pleasant tension | Sharp pain, pins-and-needles, dizziness |
| **Bands** | Easy enough for clean form; light burn OK | Form collapses, joint pain, breath-holding strain |
| **Breathing** | Exhale on effort; keep breathing easy | Forced breath-holding through sets |

---

## How the layers stack (example hour)

Rough mental model for a continuous work block:

- **:00** — work  
- **:20** — Layer A (eyes + posture)  
- **:40** — Layer B (stand circuit; this also covers the A reset window)  
- **:60** — Layer A  
- **:80** — Layer B  
- **:90** — Layer C (bands or walk; resets the longer cycle)

Exact timers may drift; when in doubt, prefer **doing the next short reset** over perfect clock alignment.

---

## Quick form cues

- **Chin tuck:** double chin, eyes level—not looking at your lap.  
- **Scap squeezes:** blades toward the spine and slightly down; neck stays quiet.  
- **Pull-aparts / face pulls / rows:** ribs down, squeeze shoulder blades, control the return.  
- **Hip flexor stretch:** tall posture; stretch in the front of the hip, not a big low-back arch.  
- **Good-mornings:** soft knees, hinge at hips, long spine—stop before form rounds hard.

## For implementers

- **Layer A** = ~25 min *active desk time*, soft eyes prompt.  
- **Layer B** = ~40 min *active desk time*, firm change + move.  
- **Layer C** = ~90 min *active desk time*, firm walk / bands.  
- **Layer S** (standing desk) = ~30 min in posture, switch sit↔stand; min dwell ~15 min.  
- **Layer D** = optional bookends / lunch, not required for core timers.  
- When B or C fires, it may absorb a concurrent A window rather than stacking back-to-back micro-prompts.  
- Never auto-prescribe aggressive stretches, neck manipulation, or “hold perfect posture” messaging.

### Activity awareness (no banked “debt”)

Wall-clock + sticky debt are wrong: **leaving the keyboard is often already the break.**  
Do not cash old timers when someone returns from a walk or sits through a video.

| Rule | Default | Why |
|------|---------|-----|
| **Active-seconds clocks** | Accrue only while input in last ~90 s | Pure AFK doesn't burn timers |
| **No prompts while passive** | Idle ≥ ~45 s → suppress all overlays | Video / reading / staring — don't hijack |
| **Natural pause for firm** | Only idle **2.5 s–45 s** while still in an active bout | Mid-keystroke defer; deep idle ≠ fire window |
| **Movement credit** | Idle ≥ ~4 min → forgive Eyes + Change + Switch | Short walk / stand-up *is* the Change |
| **Walk credit** | Idle ≥ ~8 min → forgive all layers | Real walk *is* the Walk |
| **Firm grace** after launch / wake / workday open / return from credited away | ~8 min | Settle in; debt already forgiven |
| **Posture settle** after sit/stand change | ~12 min active | Don't demand switch the second you sit |
| **Min dwell** (standing desk) | ~15 min | Avoid thrashing posture |
| **Work window edge** | Reset clocks + grace at day open | No 07:00 ambush |

Priority when due: **C > S > B > A**. Soft A never full-screens; firm layers take over the display.  
**Debt is continuous sedentary desk-work**, not a balance sheet across breaks.

This protocol prioritizes **sustainable micro-movement** for people who will actually do it between meetings and deep work—not a gym session at the desk.
